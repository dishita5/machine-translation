# Qualitative Translation Examples

## 1. Overview

This document presents representative English-to-Spanish translations generated by the Basic Transformer and TinyLlama 1.1B models.

The examples were selected from their recorded 1,000-example test prediction files. They illustrate differences in translation accuracy, readability, repetition, and handling of unknown tokens.

Individual prediction files for mT5-small, OPUS-MT, and Gemma 2B IT were not available for this qualitative comparison. Their quantitative evaluation results are reported separately in `final_model_comparison_detailed.csv`.

## 2. Translation Examples

### Example 1: Meaning Preservation

**English input:** I know what the lies were.

**Reference:** Sé cuáles eran las mentiras.

- **Basic Transformer:** `lo que lo que lo que lo que lo que lo que...`
- **TinyLlama:** Siento lo que fueron las mentiras.

**Observation:**

The Basic Transformer generated a repetitive sequence that failed to preserve the source meaning. TinyLlama produced a readable sentence, but its wording changed the intended meaning of the original English input. This illustrates that grammatical-looking output does not necessarily guarantee translation accuracy.

### Example 2: Idiomatic Expression

**English input:** This is not possible for a man of flesh and blood.

**Reference:** Esto no es posible para un hombre de carne y hueso.

- **Basic Transformer:** `esto no es una <unk> y no es una <unk>`
- **TinyLlama:** No es posible para un hombre de sangre y carne.

**Observation:**

The Basic Transformer generated unknown tokens and an incomplete translation, indicating difficulty producing the required vocabulary and sentence structure. TinyLlama preserved the general meaning, although its rendering of the idiomatic expression differed from the reference.

### Example 3: Short Conversational Sentence

**English input:** - Want help?

**Reference:** - ¿Quieres ayuda?

- **Basic Transformer:** `- <unk>`
- **TinyLlama:** - ¿Quieres ayuda?

**Observation:**

TinyLlama reproduced the reference translation accurately. In contrast, the Basic Transformer generated an unknown token and failed to produce a meaningful translation.

## 3. Discussion

The examples illustrate differences in output quality between the two available prediction sets.

The custom Basic Transformer frequently generated `<unk>` tokens and repetitive sequences. These outputs suggest limitations in its learned vocabulary representations and sequence generation under the experimental configuration.

TinyLlama generated more readable translations and correctly translated some short sentences. However, its outputs were not always semantically equivalent to the source sentence or reference translation.

The examples also demonstrate why translation quality should not be judged solely by readability. A translation may appear fluent while changing or losing part of the original meaning.

These examples are qualitative illustrations and are not a substitute for quantitative evaluation across the full test set. BLEU and chrF results for all five systems are provided separately in the main comparison results.

## 5. Conclusion

The selected examples show that the Basic Transformer struggled with repetition, unknown tokens, and incomplete translations. TinyLlama generally produced more readable outputs, although some translations did not fully preserve the original meaning.

These qualitative observations are consistent with the broader experimental comparison, in which TinyLlama achieved higher BLEU and chrF scores than the Basic Transformer.

However, conclusions about the other evaluated systems are based on their quantitative results because their individual prediction files were unavailable for this analysis.
